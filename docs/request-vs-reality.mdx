# 🔮 Request vs. Reality 🛠️

---

Stakeholder ambiguity is a common challenge in any environment. Requests often arrive as vague questions or broad asks, leaving analysts and engineers to interpret the underlying business need.

When building data products or reporting frameworks, stakeholders often request metrics based on historical habits rather than underlying business needs. 

This matrix outlines how I bridge the gap between ambiguous requests and actionable data solutions.

## Request Triage & Decision Flow

Even though we touched on the triage process in the [intake flow](how-work-comes-in) page, this section continues to show the the lifecycle of how I translate stakeholder requests into actionable data products.

  ## Decoding Ambiguous Requests

<div className="requestRealityTable">

| Ambiguous Stakeholder Request | Probing & Clarifying Questions (The Diagnostic Step) | The Messy Reality (Why Raw Data Fails) | The True Underlying Need | The Solution / What Is Delivered |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"Can you pull a report of all support tickets where customers were frustrated last week?"** | • What specific indicators define "frustrated"—explicit escalation tags, negative CSAT ratings, or keyword triggers?<br />• Are we evaluating this across all support tiers, or targeting a specific line of business? | Sentiment flags are inconsistently applied by frontline agents, and definitions of "frustrated" vary significantly across regional and product queues. | Leadership needs visibility into operational friction points and systemic product issues causing customer escalations, not just a raw volume count. | An automated analytical data model that groups interactions by root-cause category and escalation flags, paired with an executive trend visualization. |
| **"We need a dashboard showing exactly how many active accounts each account manager owns."** | • How are we defining "active"—a 30-day interaction window, a revenue threshold, or active ticket flow?<br />• How should the model handle parent-subsidiary organizational hierarchies or multi-branded account structures? | "Active" is loosely defined by stakeholders; dormant accounts, shared parent organizations, and multi-entity setups heavily distort raw counts. | Management requires an objective, data-driven way to measure workload balance and capacity constraints before distributing new account loads. | A weighted complexity scoring model (incorporating volume, tier value, and multi-entity flags) mapping accounts to actual operational capacity slots. |
| **"Can you add a standard performance target for advisor satisfaction scores next quarter?"** | • Should this target adapt to different interaction types (synchronous chats vs. complex deep-dive cases), or is it a flat team-wide goal?<br />• How do we handle low-volume queues where a single outlier score heavily skews the average? | Historical baseline data is fragmented across long-format transactional tables, and unhandled interaction type splits break the mathematical baseline. | Operations requires reliable, auditable coaching targets that accurately reflect performance realities across diverse operational workflows. | A documented transformation pipeline featuring rigorous null-handling, fallback floors, and pre-computed metric splits for fair evaluation. |
