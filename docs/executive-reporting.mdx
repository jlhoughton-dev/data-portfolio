# Executive Reporting

	<div className="workforceBadge">From data to decisions</div>

<div className="workforceSectionCard">

## 🧭 Situation

Operational leaders needed to understand how support workload and portfolio performance varied across representatives and teams. The underlying data contained useful signals, but those signals were spread across employee, customer, and invoice records.

Without a connected view, it was difficult to see workload balance and performance together.

</div>

<div className="workforceSectionCard">

## 🎯 Task

The goal was to turn the modeled data into reporting that could help stakeholders quickly evaluate capacity, coverage, and revenue performance.

The reporting needed to make complex operational relationships easier to compare and communicate without requiring every audience member to work through the underlying SQL.

</div>

<div className="workforceSectionCard">

## 🛠️ Action

I connected the domain model to an executive reporting experience designed around the questions leaders need to answer.

This included:

- bringing capacity, workload, and revenue measures into one reporting view
- framing metrics around support representative and team-level performance
- surfacing customer coverage, orders handled, portfolio revenue, and revenue per account
- organizing the output so workload balance and performance patterns could be reviewed together
- documenting the path from raw records through modeling and into business-facing reporting

The emphasis was on giving the numbers enough context to support a useful conversation, not simply displaying more metrics.

</div>

<div className="workforceSectionCard">

## 📈 Result

The reporting foundation made it easier to communicate operational performance at a glance and created a shared language for conversations about staffing, workload, and revenue generation.

By connecting the dashboard back to documented SQL models, the reporting became easier to explain, trace, and use as a decision-support tool.

</div>
